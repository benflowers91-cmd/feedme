# EOL Message

**Type:** Component
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/eol-message/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

Craft an empathetic End-of-Life announcement for a discontinued product or feature. Customer-centric communication that acknowledges loss while framing the change as progress — not a generic shutdown notice.

---

## Nine-Component Framework

1. **Company context** — establish who you are
2. **Announcement** — what's discontinuing and what replaces it
3. **Rationale** — customer-facing benefits (not "reduces costs" — say "allows us to invest in features you requested")
4. **Product context** — what's being retired
5. **Customer impact** — acknowledge disruption honestly
6. **Transition solution** — the replacement with concrete improvements
7. **Support measures** — migration assistance details
8. **Timeline** — specific dates and milestones (not "soon")
9. **Call to action** — clear next steps and contact info

---

## Critical Success Factors

- **Gather complete context first** — don't announce without: product details, replacement solution, timeline, customer impact scope, support plan
- **Acknowledge disruption explicitly** — address workflows affected, learning curves, integration changes
- **Provide sufficient lead time** — 6–12 months typical; vague timelines trigger anxiety and churn

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Centring business rationale instead of customer benefits
- Using imprecise language like "soon" rather than specific dates
- Offering no migration support or training
- Skipping the impact acknowledgment section
- Using apologetic or blaming tone

Customers will ask "What do I do now?" — you must have complete answers before communicating.
