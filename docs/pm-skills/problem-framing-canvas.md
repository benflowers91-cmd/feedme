# Problem Framing Canvas

**Type:** Component
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/problem-framing-canvas/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

Ensures you're solving the right problem before jumping to solutions. Use this before designing any new FeedMe feature — especially when a problem feels obvious, because that's when confirmation bias is hardest to notice.

Best for: clarifying messy problems, surfacing hidden assumptions, realigning on problem definition before building.

---

## Three Phases

### Phase 1: Look Inward
- What are the symptoms of the problem?
- What barriers exist to solving it?
- What assumptions is the team making? What biases might be in play?

### Phase 2: Look Outward
- Who experiences this problem, and what are the consequences for them?
- Who else has a similar problem?
- Who's been left out of the conversation? Who benefits from the status quo?

### Phase 3: Reframe
- Synthesise into a refined problem statement
- Convert to an actionable "How Might We…" question

---

## Template

```
PROBLEM SYMPTOMS
[What we observe]

BARRIERS
[What's stopping us from solving it]

ASSUMPTIONS & BIASES
[What we're taking for granted]

WHO EXPERIENCES THIS
[Affected parties and consequences]

WHO'S LEFT OUT
[Voices missing from the conversation]

WHO BENEFITS FROM STATUS QUO
[Interests that resist change]

REFINED PROBLEM STATEMENT
[One sentence]

HOW MIGHT WE...
[Actionable question to take into ideation]
```

---

## Common Pitfalls

- **Using it as a solution brainstorm** — stay in problem space until Phase 3
- **Solo exercise** — needs diverse perspectives to challenge groupthink
- **Skipping Phase 2** — the people most affected by a problem often aren't in the room
