# Discovery Process

**Type:** Workflow
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/discovery-process/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

A structured 6-phase discovery cycle (3–4 weeks) moving from problem hypothesis to validated solution. Run 1–2 cycles per quarter. Maintain at least one customer interview per week to keep discovery continuous rather than a one-time event.

---

## Six Phases

| Phase | Days | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Frame the Problem | 1–2 | Problem hypothesis, 3–5 research questions |
| 2. Research Planning | 3 | Interview design, 5–10 participants recruited |
| 3. Conduct Research | Weeks 1–2 | Interview recordings, analytics review |
| 4. Synthesise Insights | End of week 2 | Affinity map, prioritised pain points |
| 5. Generate & Validate Solutions | Week 3 | Opportunity solution tree, experiments |
| 6. Decide & Document | End of week 3–4 | GO/PIVOT/KILL decision, PRD if proceeding |

---

## Key Success Factors

- **Interview quality matters most** — use Mom Test approach: past behaviour, not hypotheticals
- **Reach saturation** — continue until patterns repeat across 3+ participants (typically 5–7 interviews)
- **Time-box ruthlessly** — 3–4 weeks maximum; analysis paralysis defeats discovery's purpose

---

## When to Use

- Exploring new product areas
- Investigating retention problems
- Validating strategic initiatives
- Establishing continuous discovery

**Avoid when:** problems are well-understood, stakeholders have pre-committed to solutions, or addressing tactical bugs.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Skipping customer interviews entirely
- Asking leading questions that invite confirmation bias
- Stopping after 2–3 interviews
- Spending weeks analysing without moving to solutions
- Treating discovery as a pre-development phase rather than an ongoing practice
