# Jobs-to-be-Done

**Type:** Component
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/jobs-to-be-done/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

Systematically uncovers what customers actually need to accomplish — their functional, social, and emotional objectives — along with the pains they experience and gains they seek. Reveals unmet needs and validates product-market fit.

Use during early-stage discovery, product-market fit validation, roadmap prioritisation, competitive analysis, and messaging development.

---

## Three Categories of Customer Jobs

**Functional Jobs** — specific tasks to perform: "send invoices," "track expenses," "adapt a recipe"

**Social Jobs** — how people wish to be perceived: appearing organised, looking tech-savvy, seeming competent

**Emotional Jobs** — desired psychological states: feeling confident, avoiding anxiety, achieving a sense of accomplishment

Social and emotional jobs often drive adoption more powerfully than functional ones.

---

## Pain Categories

- **Challenges** — obstacles blocking job completion
- **Costliness** — what demands excessive time, money, or effort
- **Common Mistakes** — preventable errors customers regularly make
- **Unresolved Problems** — gaps current solutions don't address

---

## Gain Categories

- **Expectations** — what would exceed current solutions
- **Savings** — quantified efficiencies (hours saved, costs reduced)
- **Adoption Factors** — switching drivers (free trials, migration assistance)
- **Life Improvement** — broader benefits beyond the immediate task

---

## Critical Distinctions

**Jobs are not feature requests.** "I want AI and dashboards" describes solutions, not underlying needs.

**Jobs are not demographics.** "Millennials prefer mobile-first" is a persona trait, not a job.

**The job-solution separation:** "Communicate with my team" (job) vs. "email" (solution). This separation reveals unexpected competitors — customers might "hire" spreadsheets or pen and paper instead of your product.

---

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing jobs with solutions — ask "why?" repeatedly until you reach root motivation
- Generic framing — "save time" only becomes actionable when specified: "reduce monthly report generation from 8 hours to 1 hour"
- Ignoring emotional and social dimensions
- Assumption-based analysis without customer research — ground all JTBD in actual conversations
- Treating all pains equally — explicitly rank by intensity and impact
