# Discovery Interview Prep

**Type:** Interactive
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/discovery-interview-prep/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

Structured preparation for customer discovery interviews. Adapts methodology based on your research goal, customer access, and constraints. Produces a complete interview plan with questions, bias checklist, and success criteria.

**Not user testing. Not sales demos. Not surveys.** This is deep qualitative research (5–10 people).

---

## Four-Question Flow

**Q1: Research Goal** — Problem validation / JTBD discovery / churn investigation / feature prioritisation

**Q2: Target Segment** — Be specific. Not "small business owners" but "owners manually processing 50+ invoices weekly who've tried Stripe and switched away."

**Q3: Constraints** — Limited customer access? Cold outreach? Internal stakeholders only? Existing customer base? Methodology adjusts.

**Q4: Methodology** — Mom Test-style / JTBD interviews / switch interviews / timeline mapping

---

## Output: Structured Interview Plan

- Opening script with rapport-building and consent
- 5 core questions with rationale, follow-ups, and what NOT to ask
- Bias checklist
- Success criteria (specific stories, past behaviour, patterns across 3+ interviews)
- Recruitment and logistics

---

## Bias Mitigation

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| "Don't you think X is a problem?" | "Tell me about your experience with X" |
| "Would you use this?" | "What have you tried? Why did it work or fail?" |
| "Is invoicing hard?" | "Walk me through your invoicing process" |

---

## Five Major Pitfalls

1. Asking what customers *want* instead of uncovering past behaviour
2. Pitching disguised as research — explaining your solution early
3. Interviewing the wrong segment (friends, non-users, people without the problem)
4. Stopping at 1–2 interviews and treating one-off feedback as patterns
5. Not recording insights immediately after conversations
