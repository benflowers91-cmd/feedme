# Stakeholder Engagement Advisor

**Type:** Interactive
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/stakeholder-engagement-advisor/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

Per-stakeholder engagement planning. Diagnoses each relationship through three questions and delivers a customised strategy: key message framing, recommended medium and cadence, and what you need from them vs. what they need from you.

Use for high-stakes stakeholders — those who can block, delay, or reshape your initiative. Don't run detailed engagement plans on every stakeholder.

---

## Three Diagnostic Questions

1. **Stakeholder Profile** — executive sponsor / cross-functional peer / end user / skeptic or blocker / newly identified
2. **Power and Impact** — their organisational authority vs. how deeply the initiative affects them (high/low on each dimension)
3. **Engagement Context** — first contact / preparing for a milestone / addressing resistance / elevating marginalised voices / maintaining alignment

---

## Key Insight: Bidirectional Analysis

Map what the stakeholder needs, wants, and fears — *separately* from what you need from them. Effective engagement serves both directions simultaneously.

---

## Deliverables

- Key message (the single most important understanding to establish)
- Framing (how the initiative connects to *their* goals)
- Medium and format (email / 1:1 / briefing / research participation)
- Cadence (one-time or recurring)
- Named next action with owner, medium, deadline, and success criteria

---

## Common Mistakes

- Planning only your communication goals, not their motivations
- Using proxy stakeholders who are convenient rather than credible
- Ending without specific next-action accountability
- Applying identical engagement mediums across different stakeholder types
- Treating engagement plans as static rather than evolving with the relationship

---

## Equity Note

High-impact, low-power stakeholders need proactive outreach. An open-door policy only serves people who already know they have a seat at the table.
