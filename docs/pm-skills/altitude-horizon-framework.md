# Altitude-Horizon Framework

**Type:** Component
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/altitude-horizon-framework/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

A mental model for the PM-to-Director transition. Maps the shift in thinking across two dimensions: **altitude** (scope) and **horizon** (time). Use this to understand where you are in the transition and what specifically needs to change.

---

## The Two Axes

**Altitude (Scope):**
- PM: individual customer problems, sprint priorities
- Director: portfolio strategy, cross-functional systems, organisational dynamics

**Horizon (Time):**
- PM: days, weeks, quarters
- Director: quarters to annual and multi-year, working backward from future states

---

## The Waiter vs. Restaurant Operator

| | PM (Waiter) | Director (Operator) |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Individual experience | Entire system — staffing, margins, menu |
| Authority | Influence without control | Portfolio decisions, budgets, resources |
| Success | Table seven is happy | Restaurant is profitable and scalable |
| Failure mode | Ignoring individual needs | Over-obsessing on one customer |

---

## Four Critical Transition Zones

1. **Thinking Altitude** — from solving individual problems directly to designing systems that solve classes of problems
2. **Persona Shift** — from individual user personas to buyer personas, market cohorts, organisational stakeholders
3. **Hero Syndrome Recovery** — replace personal problem-solving wins with satisfaction from team development
4. **Direction Creation** — stop waiting for clarity; start creating context cascades that translate company strategy downward

---

## The Cascading Context Map

When organisational direction is vague, Directors don't wait — they create clarity:
1. Extract 3–5 key priorities from leadership communications
2. Translate into business unit objectives
3. Map product portfolio contributions
4. Define team-specific accountabilities
5. Communicate the cascade, showing connections upward

---

## Named Failure Modes

- **Hero Syndrome** — jumping into tactical work, creating team dependency
- **Allergic to Process** — letting high performers operate independently, creating silos
- **People-Pleaser Leadership** — prioritising being liked over hard decisions
- **Instant Gratification Trap** — trying to shortcut experience through study and certifications
- **Black-and-White Thinking** — expecting clear solutions in inherently ambiguous situations
