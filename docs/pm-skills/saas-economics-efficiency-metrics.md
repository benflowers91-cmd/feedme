# SaaS Economics & Efficiency Metrics

**Type:** Component

## Purpose

Framework for evaluating whether a SaaS business model is financially viable and capital-efficient. Use for fundraising preparation, board reporting, and strategic investment decisions.

See `finance-metrics-quickref.md` for fast lookups. This skill provides detailed calculation guidance with examples.

---

## Unit Economics (Customer-Level Profitability)

**Gross Margin** = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100
- Benchmark: 70–85% good; <60% concerning
- COGS includes hosting, infrastructure, payment processing, onboarding

**CAC** = Total S&M Spend / New Customers Acquired
- Enterprise: $10K+ acceptable; SMB: <$500 target
- Include marketing, sales salaries, tools, commissions

**LTV (Better Formula)** = ARPU × Gross Margin % / Monthly Churn Rate
- Must be 3× CAC minimum

**LTV:CAC** = LTV / CAC
- 3:1 healthy; <1:1 unsustainable; >5:1 may be underinvesting in growth

**Payback Period** = CAC / (Monthly ARPU × Gross Margin %)
- <12 months excellent; 12–18 acceptable; >24 concerning
- Fast payback beats high LTV:CAC if cash is constrained

**Contribution Margin** = (Revenue - All Variable Costs) / Revenue × 100
- Includes COGS plus support, processing, variable customer success
- Benchmark: 60–80%

---

## Capital Efficiency

**Rule of 40** = Revenue Growth % + Profit Margin %
- >40 healthy; 25–40 acceptable; <25 concerning

**Magic Number** = (Current Q Revenue - Previous Q Revenue) × 4 / Previous Q S&M Spend
- >0.75 efficient; 0.5–0.75 acceptable; <0.5 fix before scaling

**Runway** = Cash Balance / Monthly Net Burn
- >12 months healthy; 6–12 manageable; <6 crisis
- Fundraise at 6–9 months runway, not 3

---

## The Hidden Cash Trap (Example)

A company with 5:1 LTV:CAC and 36-month payback looks great on paper but will run out of cash before recovering acquisition costs. Always pair LTV:CAC with payback period.

---

## Critical Pitfalls

1. High LTV:CAC without payback analysis
2. Using revenue instead of profit in LTV (always include gross margin)
3. Scaling S&M when Magic Number <0.5
4. Ignoring cohort-level data (blended metrics hide segment problems)
5. Celebrating Rule of 40 while burning rapidly with <3 months runway

Always calculate metrics separately by customer segment — blended averages hide critical trends.
