# AI Recommendation Canvas

**Type:** Component
**Source:** [deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills](https://github.com/deanpeters/Product-Manager-Skills/blob/main/skills/recommendation-canvas/SKILL.md)

---

## Purpose

Evaluates AI product ideas by assessing business outcomes, customer value, problem framing, solution hypotheses, positioning, risks, and justification. Transforms AI solution proposals from speculative pitches into defensible strategic arguments.

Use when pitching new AI capabilities requiring significant resources, aligning cross-functional teams, or building investment cases.

---

## Ten Canvas Sections

1. **Business Outcome** — Revenue, retention, or efficiency metric benefiting the company
2. **Product Outcome** — Customer-centric benefit, written from user perspective
3. **Problem Statement** — Persona-grounded narrative of the actual challenge
4. **Solution Hypothesis** — If/then structure with experiments and validation measures
5. **Positioning Statement** — Value proposition and competitive differentiation
6. **Assumptions & Unknowns** — Explicit list of what could invalidate the thesis
7. **PESTEL Risks** — Political, economic, social, technological, environmental, legal factors
8. **Value Justification** — Executive-level reasoning (data-driven, not sentiment)
9. **Success Metrics** — SMART measures tracking impact after launch
10. **Next Steps** — Immediate priorities for validation and decision-making

---

## Outcome Definition Discipline

**Business outcomes:** "[Direction] [Metric] [Outcome] [Context] [Acceptance Criteria]"
Example: "Increase by 15% monthly recurring revenue from enterprise customers within 12 months."

**Product outcomes:** Same structure from the user's perspective.
"Reduce invoice processing time by 60%" describes an outcome. "Implement AI automation" describes a tactic.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Vague outcomes** — lack measurability and accountability
- **Solution-first framing** — problem statement starts with "We need AI-powered X"
- **Skipped experimentation** — rushing from hypothesis to roadmap without testing assumptions
- **Generic risk analysis** — PESTEL becomes theatre without specific, testable assertions
- **Weak justification** — "customers will like this" instead of data-driven reasoning
